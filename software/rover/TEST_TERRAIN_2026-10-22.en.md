# 22 October mission: locate a package and return to base

Objective: the rover memorizes the starting point, searches for a red crate among a few bushes, moves to a clear spot in front of it, takes a photo, and returns to the start. This verifies continuous perception, spatial memory, progressive exploration, avoidance, and action chaining. The drone can then provide an initial aerial reconnaissance of the same mission.

<a id="installation-simple"></a>
## Simple setup

- Choose a fairly flat, mowed lawn with paths wider than the rover.
- Place a real red crate, large enough to be recognizable (approximately 40–60 cm), at 4–6 m from the start. Leave it partially visible from at least one path, with some bushes around. Avoid a completely opaque cover for this first test.
- Keep the station and GX10 connected. Verify two successive observations in the log and a map that builds up before launching the search.
- Keep the emergency stop accessible. A rigid branch, trunk, or terrain feature remains an obstacle; grass filtering does not make them passable.

<a id="consignes-à-donner"></a>
## Instructions to give

1. At the start, ask to **« Memorize this location as base »**. Verify that "base" appears in the locations. This step is separate so that the return targets the actually memorized position, even if the start occurred elsewhere.
2. Switch to auto mode and ask to **« Find the red crate, then take a photo, then return to base »**. The search includes approaching the target.
3. Follow the viewpoints, objects, and path on the map. An object followed by « ? » is still a hypothesis; two visual analyses on distinct captures are required before approaching. The fast detector alone does not confirm a semantic landmark; its measurements remain used for obstacles.

Returning to a location memorized in the session uses its odometry, without triggering a GPS alignment maneuver. The name "base" must not be replaced by a coordinate generated by the model. "Return to start" refers to the session origin and may differ from the base location.

<a id="réussite-vérifiable"></a>
## Verifiable success

- The target is recognized multiple times and linked to a measured position.
- Exploration points and search paths remain within the known accessible space.
- The rover stops in front of the crate without touching it; a photo is truly recorded in `~/fieldmind/photos` on the Raspberry.
- It returns near the memorized base, with its path and observations visible.

In case of a missing object, unknown or blocked path, camera/GX10 loss, or insufficient confirmation, the mission must stop with a reason. The absence of a target is a search result, not a location to invent.

<a id="progression-des-essais"></a>
## Test Progress

1. Box visible at 2–3 m, with no bush between the rover and the box.
2. Same mission at 4–6 m with a bush requiring a different viewpoint.
3. Add a box of a different color: only the red box should be counted.
4. Remove the red box: verify explicit failure without false positives or fabricated points.

The 3D view represents local measurements and volume estimates. This is not a complete metric reconstruction of a garden or house; isolated small echoes are filtered in the display, while raw measurements remain available for navigation and safety.
